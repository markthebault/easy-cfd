# Track lab POC

Open `?workspace=track` or choose Track lab from the workspace menu. Space pauses the car and flow. F switches follow/free camera. Free camera supports orbit, right-drag pan, wheel zoom, WASD movement and Q/E down/up while paused. The lap slider pauses and seeks. Road, verge, kerbs and terrain fade to 5.5% opacity with depth writing disabled when the camera is below the car's road plane.

## Track and car placement

Three GP GeoJSON snapshots are pinned from open-racetrack-db PR #10, commit `3a1381ae5fca0963c369bca71e7f3d5fc448ccb1`. Refresh after merge using `node scripts/sync-track-data.mjs main`. Original metadata and feature licenses remain in the snapshots. Rendering uses the trace at nominal 4 m stations. Flat road is the default. The optional Estimated elevation mode uses supplied terrain-raster heights with periodic, shape-preserving cubic interpolation, closed at start/finish. These approximately 79 m raster estimates are not road-survey heights. Estimated borders are retained but not rendered. Road width is nominally 12 m and kerb placement is schematic, not surveyed.

The raw OSM spline is integrated span by span at nominal 0.25 m spacing before resampling in metres. Elevation is assigned by measured cumulative distance, never sample index. The prior fixed 200-entry lookup compressed some nominal 4 m stations below 0.3 m and produced the artificial ramps. Regression checks now cover actual station spacing and grade at every source knot. The road and car share one continuous Catmull–Rom path. The car's quaternion follows the front/rear axle contact points, including grade, rather than snapping to a new heading at each ~4 m sample. Its height uses the solved tire minima and the road surface offset. Kerbs are merged red/white swept strips in bends. The scene uses Three's atmospheric Sky dome, environment lighting and a moving car shadow. A terrain mesh follows the road corridor and blends into the source raster when estimated elevation is enabled; the distant ground is joined by a sloping skirt. This replaces the old disconnected flat floor beneath elevated road ribbons.

## Precomputed OpenFOAM lap playback

The baseline speed/yaw map is in `web/public/track-cfd/map.json` and `map/`. It contains **six independent native OpenFOAM v2412 solves**, at **40 and 240 km/h**, each at **−10°, 0°, +10° yaw**. All six use the same **39,383-cell Fast mesh** and exact same five-part MX-5 geometry. They passed `checkMesh` and residual convergence. Five settled in the force-window test; the 240 km/h / 0° sample did not, and remains marked provisional. Fast has no prism layers: this map is exploratory, not a mesh-converged aerodynamic reference.

`build-track-cfd-map.py` builds the baseline mesh once, checks the cell budget and that every part survives, and reuses it across the conditions. Every case solves its own velocity, pressure and turbulence fields with moving-road/rotating-wheel boundary conditions. The native cases, solver logs, settings and extracted results remain in `/Volumes/SSD512/persospace/easy-cfd/.easycfd/track-map-fast-20261106`. Playback files preserve float32 samples with lossless gzip and SHA-256 hashes. The display grid remains the main workspace's 900,000-point target (**909,293 samples per case**); this sampling density does not increase the physical mesh resolution.

Run the batch with the existing Python/VTK runtime and `PYTHONPATH=backend`, using `scripts/build-track-cfd-map.py`. It requires the pinned solver image and a local backend on port 8780 for the compute lease. `TRACK_MAP_QUALITY=medium` requests the finer preset; it fails rather than silently accepting inadequate prism-layer coverage. The initial Medium attempt was retained outside the public export because its layer coverage failed that gate. The default Fast mode is explicit in the exported catalog and UI. Each case has a profile-bounded whole-job timeout; completed samples can be resumed from their cache when the common mesh hash matches.

On track/setup/wind changes, the app prepares a timeline at every ~4 m track sample. Track heading is not treated as airflow yaw. Signed curvature gives a kinematic bicycle-model sideslip estimate, β = atan(½ wheelbase × curvature). The body is oriented β away from the CG travel direction. The world wind minus vehicle velocity is projected into the car's rear/right axes to obtain the CFD inlet components. Wind bearing denotes the direction air travels: 0° north, 90° east. This is an estimated sideslip model, not a tyre-dynamics simulation.

During playback:

- The four surrounding solved speed/yaw cases are selected with bilinear weights. Exact solved conditions select the corresponding case without blending. Out-of-coverage requests are clamped to the solved boundary and visibly labelled; there is no silent extrapolation.
- Surface Cp is averaged at coincident vertices using the main workspace's welds, then interpolated numerically in the vertex shader before applying the same diverging palette and lighting. It does not interpolate rendered colours.
- Live telemetry shows net CFD lift upward and downforce downward in N, updated at 10 Hz. The two readouts are opposing components of one net vertical force, so only one is positive. Each sample exports the native solver-averaged Cl/Cd, downforce, density and reference area. Playback interpolates Cl, then computes lift = ½ρAV²Cl using the full relative inlet magnitude (longitudinal map speed / cos(yaw)); it does not linearly interpolate endpoint forces in N. Exact grid conditions reproduce the native solver averages. Values freeze on pause, update with speed/yaw/wind, retain active unsettled-case warnings, and become unavailable outside map coverage. These same CFD loads now feed the lap model and live lateral-grip estimate.
- Moving airflow uses the main workspace's RK2 particle advection with four weighted, common-grid velocity samplers. The track presentation uses 400 narrow historical trails, normal alpha blending and a 0.12 visualization time scale. White is near reference air speed, blue is faster and orange is slower/wake, matching the supplied video's visual convention. This replaces the 9,216-particle additive overlay that obscured the car. Pressure and smoke weights update every rendered frame; stored trail positions retain actual advection history. The CFD velocities are not modified for appearance.
- Streamlines are retraced from the blended velocity field in a separate worker, throttled to at most twice per second. No procedural path bending or invented vortices are added. Pausing holds the car and advection; camera motion remains available.

These are **interpolated quasi-steady speed/yaw cases**, not independent CFD solves at every metre and not a transient/rotating-frame cornering solve. Turn-rate, steering and suspension-state effects would require additional CFD dimensions or genuinely turning-flow cases. Accessory configurations use their own wind-tunnel results; they never inherit the stock map.

The old `manifest.json` retains the original provisional 144 km/h / 3° saved reference and exact solved exterior geometry. If the map is absent, the viewer explicitly falls back to that static reference. `scripts/export-track-cfd.mjs` exports such a reference via the same native field/surface endpoints as the main workspace. Model credit and preparation changes are in `web/public/track-cfd/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

## Lap model

The point-mass model samples the closed curve about every 4 m, estimates curvature, computes lateral limits then propagates braking and power/traction limits around the closed lap. Base braking and cornering default to 1.1 g **at 100 km/h on the base setup**, mass 1150 kg and wheel power 118 kW. The tyre coefficient is calibrated once from the base car's calm-air, zero-yaw CFD load at 100 km/h, then shared by the base and test cars. Normal tyre load is max(0, mg − lift). Available lateral force is μ × normal load; downforce increases it, lift reduces it. The same normal-load correction applies to braking and available traction. Drag uses the selected run's Cd, area, density and relative airspeed², so an aero part can trade cornering gains against straight-line drag.

The stock car uses interpolated coefficients from the six-case map. Outside its envelope the lap model explicitly uses boundary coefficients scaled by current dynamic pressure; live force readouts are withheld and the coverage limitation is shown. An imported single-condition wind-tunnel run uses fixed Cl/Cd with speed² scaling; this is labelled as an estimate, without claiming yaw-dependent CFD for unsolved conditions. Both cars are solved around the same track with identical mechanical settings and wind. Telemetry shows current lateral demand, available lateral capacity in g/kN, and the gain in g/N over the base car **at the same operating point**. Changes to setup/wind preserve the current road position instead of jumping to another location under the new lap timing.

The model omits tyre load sensitivity, axle balance limits, combined braking/cornering, gearing, bank benefit and elevation forces. It explores setup effects and does not predict qualified real lap times.

## Wind-tunnel setup workflow

1. Open a completed car result in **3D wind tunnel**, then choose **Test on track**. The current saved run's enabled geometry, wall Cp and velocity field are passed directly to the track renderer, with its own force coefficients and source/convergence information.
2. Choose **Use test as base** for the reference car. When saved weight-assessment mass is available it is adopted for the shared comparison.
3. Return to the wind tunnel, add/enable parts and solve that configuration. Choose **Test on track** again to compare the modified car against the pinned base. The original base snapshot remains unchanged. Base grip, mass, power, wind, circuit and elevation choice survive workspace changes.

The old generic wing/splitter/diffuser checkboxes and their assumed force bonuses have been removed. Parts are now the ones in the solved wind-tunnel snapshot. A modified result does not reuse the stock-car flow map. With one saved condition, forces scale with current airspeed² while Cp/flow shape remain at the solved yaw; particle advection speed follows the airspeed ratio. The source card states that limitation. Generating a new speed/yaw map for each package is a separate CFD batch; the transfer does not silently launch or fabricate one.

**Return to stock MX-5** restores the supplied six-case car and resets both base/test sources. Run selections and track preferences are retained for the current app session; browser reload starts with the stock car.

## Validation and preview

- `node --test web/tests/track-lab.test.mjs`: data hashes and sizes for all six real cases, full field sampling resolution, speed/yaw weights and bounds, heading-independent yaw and wind signs, native force normalization, signed lift/downforce and V² scaling, base-at-100-km/h calibration, increased cornering capacity/lap speed from downforce, exact wind-tunnel geometry/field transfer, numerical limitations retained, and continuous position/quaternion across every track sample and start/finish seam on all three tracks.
- `EASYCFD_WEB_TEST_PORT=5211 npx --prefix web playwright test --config web/playwright.config.ts web/tests/e2e/track-lab.spec.ts`: real WebGL, sky, merged kerbs, solved layers, pause freeze including force readouts, free camera below road, depth-write/opacity restoration, tire contact raycast against the actual road at five Barcelona lap positions (within 25 mm), changing wind and map weights/streamlines/forces while paused with fixed road position, all three track switches without page errors, and a base/modified wind-tunnel transfer workflow. Transfer tests use explicitly synthetic in-memory completed-run fixtures; their numbers are not published CFD evidence.
- `map-particles.spec.ts` checks actual GPU advection against a known weighted velocity and verifies that ordinary playback and pause are unchanged. `flow-smoke.spec.ts` retains the existing main-workspace smoke regression checks.
- `npm run build --prefix web`: production TypeScript/Vite build.

`airflow-motion.spec.ts` holds the car and camera still, measures visible pixel changes from CFD advection, verifies a sparse overlay, and verifies pixel-identical pause.

Tailscale preview is managed by `local.easycfd.track-preview` LaunchAgent on loopback 5208, HTTPS 8457. Existing Serve routes are preserved. The Barcelona video is copied into the served build under `demo/barcelona.mp4` after building.
